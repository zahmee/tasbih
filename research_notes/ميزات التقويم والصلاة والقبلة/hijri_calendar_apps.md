## 2. Which Hijri computation each uses, ±day shifts, following a country's announcement, and month boundaries

### Takeaway
Almost every Arabic-market app says it uses Umm al-Qura. The better ones add a manual ±1/±2 day correction. Only one app, Yahya Alzahrani's (iOS listing), claims to detect the user's country and follow that country's official announcement automatically.

Umm al-Qura is officially a pre-computed civil calendar. Religious months are set by sighting, and in 2026 the two differed: Saudi Dhul-Qa'dah 1447 ran one day off the computed calendar, and Egypt started Ramadan a day after Saudi Arabia. An app that only computes Umm al-Qura will be "wrong by one day" for some users in some months.

### Cited Findings
**How Umm al-Qura works (official basis)**
- The current rule has applied since 1423 AH (15 Mar 2002). On the 29th day of a month, the next day starts a new month only if (1) the geocentric conjunction happens before sunset and (2) the moon sets after the sun, with Mecca (the Great Mosque) as the reference point. Otherwise the month runs 30 days. — [R. van Gent, Utrecht University, "Umm al-Qura rules"](https://webspace.science.uu.nl/~gent0113/islam/ummalqura_rules.htm)
- KACST's Institute of Astronomical & Geophysical Research computes the dates, and the calendar is "used by the government of Saudi Arabia for civil purposes". — [van Gent](https://webspace.science.uu.nl/~gent0113/islam/ummalqura_rules.htm)
- Under the current rule, about 75% of month-start crescents are not visible to the naked eye. — [van Gent](https://webspace.science.uu.nl/~gent0113/islam/ummalqura_rules.htm)
- Earlier rule periods:
  - 1392–1419 AH: a rule based on the conjunction time relative to Saudi midnight.
  - 1420–1422 AH: moonset after sunset only.
  - [van Gent](https://webspace.science.uu.nl/~gent0113/islam/ummalqura_rules.htm)
- Taqwemee's developer explains it in Arabic:
  - Umm al-Qura is "an astronomical calculated calendar, not a religious one".
  - Government matters use the calculated calendar.
  - Ramadan and the Eids follow sighting.
  - The two can differ by a day.
  - [xsoh.ws/hijriah (Arabic)](http://xsoh.ws/hijriah)
- Another summary says the official Umm al-Qura site warns that the calendar may not match actual crescent visibility, which religious months require. This is a secondary summary; the primary site would not load. — [search summary citing al-habib.info](https://www.al-habib.info/islamic-calendar/ummulqura.htm)

**Real 2026 boundary cases (1447 AH)**
- **Ramadan 1447.**
  - The Saudi Supreme Court called for sighting on the evening of 17 Feb 2026, which was 29 Sha'ban by Umm al-Qura. Saudi Arabia, Qatar, the UAE and others then began Ramadan on **Wed 18 Feb**. — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/ramadan/saudi-arabia-calls-on-muslims-to-sight-ramadan-crescent-on-february-17); [Greater Kashmir](https://m.greaterkashmir.com/article/crescent-sighted-ramadhan-to-begin-tomorrow-in-saudi-arabia/479506)
  - **Egypt's Dar al-Ifta** declared Wed 18 Feb the 30th of Sha'ban, so Egypt began Ramadan on **Thu 19 Feb**. — [Ahram Online](https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/562560.aspx); [Egypt Independent](https://egyptindependent.com/dar-al-ifta-observes-ramadan-crescent-moon-on-tuesday)
  - Japan's Hilal committee also chose 19 Feb. — [Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/international/ramadan-2026-moon-sighting-in-saudi-arabia-supreme-court-of-ksa-calls-on-muslims-to-search-for-crescent-on-this-date-a517/)
- **Eid al-Fitr 1447.**
  - Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Bahrain, Qatar and Kuwait celebrated on **Fri 20 Mar 2026**. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/which-countries-will-celebrate-eid-al-fitr-2026-on-friday-2-1.500479052)
  - Brunei, Singapore, Malaysia, Bangladesh and the Philippines celebrated on **Sat 21 Mar**. — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/eid-al-fitr-2026-countries-march-20-21?amp=1)
  - Reports on Turkey's date conflict.
- **Dhul-Qa'dah 1447: official date vs Umm al-Qura.** The Supreme Court ruled (Ruling No. 206/H) that Shawwal had 30 days, so 1 Dhul-Qa'dah fell on 19 Apr 2026 and Sun 17 May was the 29th. The calculated Umm al-Qura calendar listed Sun 17 May as the **30th**. That is a one-day mismatch between the official date and the computed calendar for that whole month. — [Al-Habib blog, May 2026](https://blog.al-habib.info/2026/05/saudi-arabia-calls-for-dhul-hijjah-1447-ah-moonsighting-on-sunday-may-17-high-visibility-expected/)
- **Dhul-Hijjah 1447.** After the sighting, the court set 1 Dhul-Hijjah on Mon 18 May 2026, Arafah on Tue 26 May and Eid al-Adha on Wed 27 May. This again matched Umm al-Qura, and Jordan announced the same day. — [Premium Times](https://www.premiumtimesng.com/news/more-news/880394-saudi-arabia-confirms-start-of-dhul-hijjah-1447-eid-al-adha-set-for-may-27.html); [Jordan News](https://www.jordannews.jo/Section-109/News/Jordan-s-Fatwa-Department-Monday-Marks-the-First-Day-of-Dhu-al-Hijjah-51460)

### Inferences
- Even in Saudi Arabia, computing Umm al-Qura alone is wrong for some months; Dhul-Qa'dah 1447 was one day off from the court's dates. A ±1/±2 offset, set once by the user, is the feature users expect. Offering "follow my country's announcement" needs a data feed, which only Yahya Alzahrani's app advertises.
- The ±1 or ±2 range is effectively the standard: Ibrahim Alhazmi, SocioTech, Hijri Today and Muslim Pro all describe 1–2 days.
- Google Calendar's "Hijri vs Civil Hijri" choice, and ColorOS telling users to switch between them when dates differ, show that built-in calendars offer method choice but no event-level correction. That is a gap a small app can fill.
- `ISLAMIC_UMALQURA` is built into Android, so a native app does not need its own tables. It still needs a user offset on top.

### Gaps
- I found no app documentation on whether the Hijri day changes at Maghrib (sunset) or at midnight. Hijri Today refreshes at 6 PM and midnight but does not say which rule it follows. A feature request for Maghrib-based day change exists for another app: [Locu featurebase](https://locuapp.featurebase.app/p/hijri-calendar-with-maghrib-based-day-change).
- I could not open the official KACST Umm al-Qura site (ummulqura.org.sa) for primary wording.
- I could not confirm whether Yahya Alzahrani's automatic "Adjust by sighting" exists in the Android build, or how its data is sourced.
- I could not confirm Samsung's adjustment range, Google's current variant labels on Android, or Xiaomi and Honor support (no results found).

## 3. Calendar views: grid layout, primary calendar, week start, navigation, year view, conversion

### Takeaway
The leading apps show a month grid with both dates, swipe or arrows between months, a Hijri↔Gregorian converter, and often a date-difference or age tool. Taqwemee is Hijri-primary, with one Hijri month per page. Some newer minimalist apps (maraya) skip the grid and show today's card plus a year table. I found no source on week start day or today highlight style.

### Cited Findings
- **Ibrahim Alhazmi**: shows the Hijri and Gregorian calendars, switches months "by swiping from right to left or vice versa", converts in both directions, and calculates "the difference between two dates in Hijri or Gregorian". — [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ibrahim.hijricalendar&hl=en_US&gl=SA)
- **Taqwemee**:
  - Each Hijri month is shown on its own page, and users praise that both dates are shown clearly (review 2 Jul 2026).
  - Events appear as a red line under the day; a reviewer asked for text instead (9 Dec 2024).
  - A note dot disappears at some font sizes (2020, older).
  - Another reviewer asked for the month number next to the Hijri and Gregorian month names (6 Sep 2023).
  - [Taqwemee reviews (Arabic)](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=ws.xsoh.taqwemee&hl=ar&gl=SA)
- **Yahya Alzahrani**: lists "Past and Future Years and Months Display", the converter in both directions, night mode, and Arabic and English. In 2021 (older), users said ads covered so much of the screen the whole month could not be seen, and that the app froze when moving between months with the arrows. — [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.saudicalendars.calendar_app&hl=en_US&gl=SA)
- **Tatbiqati**: shows "the entire Hijri month and year, and easily navigate between months and years", plus a converter, moon phase (illumination and age) and appointments linked to a Hijri or Gregorian date. — [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mobeasyapp.app301303511423&hl=en_US&gl=SA)
- **maraya_app**: a "Today's date card — Hijri & Gregorian" plus a "Full year table — every remaining day with both dates side by side". It is pitched as "For those who love speed & simplicity". — [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.app4.aseela&hl=en_US&gl=SA)
- **Adintop**: converter, Hijri and Gregorian age calculation, moon phases, seasons, and the Hijri and Gregorian months with a countdown of remaining days. — [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.adintop.hijriapp&hl=en_US&gl=SA)
- **SocioTech**: a dual side-by-side calendar, notes or "stationery markers" pinned to dates, a converter, and a Hijri birthday and lunar age calculator. — [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.calendar.hijri&hl=en_US&gl=SA)
- **nachit**: a month calendar with the corresponding Gregorian date, navigation between months and years, a conversion widget, and shareable date cards. — [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.hijritoday.Widgetdate&hl=en_US&gl=SA)

### Inferences
- In 2026, most leading apps treat a conversion tool and month navigation as baseline features. The newer differentiators are a year table (maraya), a date-difference tool (Ibrahim Alhazmi) and shareable date cards (nachit, DevPlus).

### Gaps

## 6. Display details: month names, digits, day names, Hijri date format

### Takeaway
Arabic-first apps use full Arabic month names and Arabic-Indic digits with the "هـ" suffix. English-UI apps use transliterated, sometimes abbreviated, names such as "Ram". Users repeatedly ask for (a) full or Arabic month names and (b) independent digit choices for the Hijri and Gregorian dates.

### Cited Findings
- **Athan's listing** writes the year with Arabic-Indic digits and the AH suffix. — [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.athan&hl=en_US&gl=SA)
- **Yahya Alzahrani (iOS)**: a choice of Arabic (Western) or Hindi (Arabic-Indic) numerals, in Arabic, English and French. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1532182932)
- **Taqwemee**: a reviewer asked for Arabic-Indic digits for the Hijri date and Western digits for the Gregorian date at the same time (3 Apr 2020, older, 20 helpful votes). The app lets users pick Arabic or English independently of the system language. — [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=ws.xsoh.taqwemee&hl=en_US&gl=SA)
- **Ibrahim Alhazmi**: a review asked for the "full month name like 'Ramdhan' instead of 'Ram'" and Arabic dates as an option (May 2020, older, 389 helpful votes). - A good default for Arabic UI is the weekday name, then the day in Arabic-Indic digits, the full Arabic month name, and the year followed by "هـ". The Gregorian line should use its own digit setting, and month numbers are optional. This matches what reviewers ask for.
- Project note (from this project's own memory, not a web source): a middle dot next to Arabic-Indic digits reads as a zero, so separators near Arabic digits should be a dash.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the exact date strings each app renders (for example, whether "هـ" or "AH" is used in widgets), because that needs screenshots.

## 9. Which app is considered best overall, best design, and most accurate for Saudi Arabia
